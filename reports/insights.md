# Insights report: Complete Journey 2017

_Generated by `python -m retail.analytics` from `sql/analysis/*.sql` and `reports/stats_results.json`. Sales are purchases by the 2,469 panel households, not total store sales._

## Headline findings

1. Households in the panel spent $4,198,540 on merchandise across 141,180 trips in 2017. Q4 was the strongest quarter ($1,100,662, +6.2% vs Q3), and the growth came from bigger baskets (+7.4% basket value) rather than more trips (-1.1%).
2. Sales are concentrated: the top 10% of households generate 34.2% of sales and the top 20% generate 53.5%.
3. About half of coupon redemptions subsidise purchases that were already happening: 50.6% (95% CI 48.5-52.8%) came from households that bought the same product in the previous 56 days, rising to 54.5% for Type A campaigns. Recommendation: exclude recent buyers of a product from that product's coupon.
4. Campaign ROI cannot be measured reliably with the current design. A difference-in-differences test against never-targeted households fails its placebo check for 3 of 3 campaign types: targeted households' spend was already falling relative to controls before launch (by up to $5.26 per household per week). Targeting is aimed at declining shoppers, so any measured 'lift' is confounded. Recommendation: hold out a random 10% of each target list.
5. In-store support compounds. Versus the same product in the same store in unpromoted weeks, a mailer alone lifts sales 131% and a display alone 129%, but both together lift them 225% (95% CI 219-230%).
6. Own-brand opportunity: Soft Drinks ($182,124) has only 11.4% private-label share although own brand is priced 46% below national brands; Frozen Pizza, Crackers/Misc Bkd Fd show the same pattern.
7. Deals drive the business: households buying 40% or more of their spend on loyalty discount make up 73% of shoppers and 73.8% of sales.
8. 318 'at risk' households (frequent shoppers who have not visited for ~26 days) hold 13.4% of annual sales: the first win-back target.
9. Among surveyed households (801 of 2,469), 125K+ income households spend 1.7x as much as under-35K households, while under-35K households buy more own brand (23.9% vs 16.8% of spend).
10. Sat afternoon baskets are the largest ($36.03 vs $28.00 on an average weekday slot), so big-basket promotions and staffing should be weighted to weekend afternoons.
11. Onions and Potatoes are bought together 4.6x more often than chance, a cross-merchandising candidate.
