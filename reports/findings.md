# Findings and recommendations

_Generated by `python -m retail.findings`. Each finding states its evidence, the recommended action, the caveat and how much weight it can bear. No financial savings or ROI are claimed: the data has no costs and campaign effects are not identified._

## 1. A small group of households drives most of the sales

**Evidence.** The top 10% of households generate 34.2% of merchandise sales. RFM 'Champions' (513 households, 21% of shoppers) generate 45.7%. 318 'At risk' households (frequent shoppers, no visit for about 26 days) still hold 13.4% of annual sales.

**Recommendation.** Make retention the priority: protect Champions and run win-back offers for At-risk households before they lapse.

**Caveat.** One year of data from a loyalty panel. RFM describes past behaviour and does not predict who will leave. Segment boundaries are quintiles, not business thresholds.

**Confidence.** High: a direct measurement of what happened

**Sources.** `sql/analysis/06_rfm_segments.sql`, `sql/analysis/07_customer_concentration.sql`

## 2. About half of coupon redemptions go to households already buying the product

**Evidence.** 50.6% of 2,046 scored redemptions (95% CI 48.5-52.8%) came from households that bought a product covered by the coupon in the previous 56 days; 54.5% for Type A campaigns.

**Recommendation.** Leave a product's recent buyers out of its coupon mailings, and test the change with a holdout before rolling it out.

**Caveat.** A habitual buyer may still buy more because of the coupon, so this does not measure lost incremental volume. It is a proxy for subsidised repeat purchases.

**Confidence.** Moderate: a proxy measure, not a direct test

**Sources.** `sql/analysis/10_coupon_incrementality.sql`, `reports/stats_results.json`

## 3. The data cannot show whether campaigns increase spending

**Evidence.** Difference-in-differences against the 910 never-targeted households fails its placebo test for 3 of 3 campaign types. Targeted households were already spending less than controls before launch, by up to $5.26 per household per week. Redemption rates differ by type (Type A 16.6%, Type B 6.5%), but redemption is not the same as extra spend.

**Recommendation.** Hold out a random 10% of every campaign's target list, so future campaigns have a valid comparison group and ROI can be measured.

**Caveat.** This does not say campaigns fail. It says the current targeting makes their effect impossible to separate from the households' existing trend.

**Confidence.** Cannot conclude: the data cannot isolate the effect

**Sources.** `src/retail/stats.py`, `reports/stats_results.json`, `docs/methodology.md`

## 4. A display and a mailer together are linked to much higher sales than either alone

**Evidence.** Against the same product in the same store in unpromoted weeks, weekly sales were 131% higher with a mailer only, 129% higher with a display only, and 225% higher with both (95% CI 219-230%, 123,761 product x store pairs).

**Recommendation.** When an item is featured in the mailer, give it an in-store display too, and run a controlled store test to confirm the combined effect.

**Caveat.** Promotions are not randomly assigned. Retailers promote items in season or already trending, so these figures are an upper bound, not a causal lift. Sales are the panel households' only.

**Confidence.** Moderate: an association, not proven cause and effect

**Sources.** `sql/analysis/11_promotion_lift.sql`, `reports/stats_results.json`

## 5. Fresh-produce and meal-ingredient categories are bought together

**Evidence.** The strongest category pairs are Onions + Potatoes (lift 4.6); Onions + Vegetables - All Others (lift 4.5); Onions + Tomatoes (lift 4.5). Lift is how many times more often a pair shares a basket than chance would predict.

**Recommendation.** Place these categories near each other or bundle them in meal-solution promotions, and track whether basket size rises.

**Caveat.** This shows the categories are bought together, not that one causes the other. Results are at category level, not for individual products.

**Confidence.** High: a direct measurement of what happened

**Sources.** `sql/models/03_segments.sql`, `sql/analysis/12_market_basket.sql`
